Where to trade for XBT: exchanges checked November 15, 2026

Quick version

  1. XBT is listed on NonKYC, SafeTrade and NeoxEX (read our NeoxEX warning first).
  2. Deposit a small test, then trade with limit orders, in pieces.
  3. Withdraw your XBT to your own wallet. Don’t leave coins on a small exchange.

XBT is the coin on the chain that split from bitcoin on August 8, 2026, when a small minority of nodes and miners kept enforcing BIP-110 (a temporary cap on non-payment data) and nearly all miners stayed on the old rules. On August 30, 2026 that chain switched its mining from SHA-256 to BLAKE2b, which is why exchanges list it as XBT, BTCB2 or “Bitcoin BLAKE2b”. Anyone who held bitcoin in their own wallet before the split already holds the same amount of XBT; you only need an exchange to get more, or to sell.

Sources: xbt.live history (updated 2026-10-05); BitcoinBIP110.org announcements (PSA-260902: first BLAKE2b block 961,640, August 30, 2026). Background: why XBT exists.

Where this fits: you need an exchange if you’re starting with dollars or with bitcoin you got after August 8, 2026 (block 961,632 or later). Those coins carry no XBT, so you trade them for XBT here, then withdraw it to your own wallet. See Start here, step 2. Set up your own XBT wallet before you buy.

Watch the ticker. On Kraken and other large exchanges, “XBT” means ordinary bitcoin. On the small exchanges below, the same fork coin shows up as XBT, BTCB2 or BIP110. Before any deposit, check that the network says Bitcoin BLAKE2b / BIP110. A BTC-looking address does not tell you which chain it’s on.

XBT was not listed on Coinbase, Kraken or Binance, according to xbt.live (updated 2026-09-27). Prices, fees and deposit status change often, and these are small, young exchanges. Check the exchange’s own status page on the day you trade.

How to trade carefully

  1. Turn on two-factor login right after you make an account. Use an authenticator app rather than SMS.
  2. Prefer a BTC pair if you’re paying with bitcoin, so you skip a stablecoin step.
  3. Use limit orders (you set the price you’re willing to pay, and the order waits until someone matches it). Order books are thin, so a large order can fill far from the price you see. Buy in pieces.
  4. Send a small test deposit first, and wait until it’s credited before sending more.
  5. Withdraw to a wallet you control when you’re done trading. See Withdraw and consolidate, and test that withdrawal with a small amount too.

Safety first

  • Never share a seed phrase or private key. No exchange, wallet support desk or “claim” site needs it. Anyone who asks is trying to steal from you. This site will never ask, and has no forms for it.
  • There is nothing to “claim”. If you held bitcoin before the split, your XBT is already at your old addresses. Fake claim sites, fake wallets and phishing links target fork-coin holders. BitcoinBIP110.org’s own advice is never to enter a bitcoin seed or private key into a claiming website.
  • Split before you move pre-split coins. A normal transaction from a pre-split wallet can be copied onto the other chain (a “replay”). XBT added an opt-in signature type, SIGHASH_UNIFIED, for chain-specific signing, but ordinary bitcoin wallets don’t use it. Protect your BTC first with our step-by-step split guide. Coins you buy on an exchange exist only on XBT, so they carry no replay risk.
  • Start small. Test every deposit, withdrawal and new wallet with an amount you can afford to lose.
  • Expect big price swings. xbt.live reports XBT reached about $1,800 in early September 2026 and fell more than 80% within weeks. Small exchanges can also pause withdrawals, delist a coin or fail.

Sources: BitcoinBIP110.org PSA-260901 and PSA-260902 (SIGHASH_UNIFIED, claim-site warning); xbt.live where to buy (price history, risks). More in our safety checklist.
