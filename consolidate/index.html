Withdraw and consolidate your XBT into one wallet

Quick version

  1. Withdraw from exchanges to a fresh receive address in your own wallet, small test first.
  2. Gather coins into one wallet only after splitting, and when fees are low.
  3. Merging coins links their addresses, so think about privacy.

The end goal of this guide: all your XBT in one wallet whose seed only you hold. This page covers the last two moves: withdrawing from exchanges, and gathering scattered coins together.

Withdraw from an exchange

What you need
  • Your XBT wallet with a fresh receive address (how to get one).
  • Your exchange login with two-factor on.
  1. Copy a receive address from your wallet. On a hardware wallet, confirm it on the device screen.
  2. On the exchange, open Withdraw for XBT (listed as XBT, BTCB2 or BIP110). Check the network says Bitcoin BLAKE2b / BIP110, not Bitcoin.
  3. Send a small test and wait for it to arrive. Look it up on mempool.guide.
  4. Withdraw the rest. Expect a withdrawal fee (NonKYC: 0.0039 BTCB2 per withdrawal, checked November 15, 2026).

Why consolidate?

Your wallet holds XBT as separate coins (UTXOs): one for each payment or withdrawal you received. Gathering them helps when:

  • You have XBT in several wallets, for example an old pre-split seed, a phone wallet and exchange withdrawals. One wallet means one seed to protect and one backup to check.
  • You have many small coins. Each coin you spend adds to a transaction’s size, so a wallet full of small coins costs more to spend later. Merging them while fees are low pays that cost once, cheaply. The Bitcoin wiki notes some wallets “consolidate inputs in times of cheap miner fees” for this reason.

The tradeoffs

  • Fees now. A consolidation is a transaction, so it costs a fee, and the more coins in it, the bigger it is. Only worth it if fees are low.
  • Privacy. Spending several coins together tells everyone watching the chain that they belong to the same owner. The Bitcoin wiki calls this the common-input-ownership heuristic and says chain-surveillance companies use it widely. If you want to keep some coins unlinked (for example, exchange withdrawals and coins from your old wallet), consolidate them separately or not at all.
  • Replay. Never consolidate coins from a seed that still guards bitcoin. Split first. After that, a wallet that signs with XBT’s unified sighash (Shrike, the Sparrow BLAKE2b build, Knots) makes the transaction invalid on BTC.

How to consolidate

What you need
  • One main XBT wallet, ideally on a hardware wallet.
  • Every other wallet you want to empty, already split from BTC.
  • A quiet moment for fees.
  1. Check fees. Open mempool.guide and look at its fee estimates (in sat/vB, satoshis per unit of transaction size; lower is cheaper). When we checked on November 15, 2026, every estimate was 1 sat/vB, the minimum. Pick a time when the low-priority estimate is lowest.
  2. Get a fresh receive address in your main wallet.
  3. Empty each other wallet into it. Send a small test first, then send everything (use the wallet’s send-all or max option). Use a low fee; you’re not in a hurry.
  4. Merge small coins inside one wallet (optional). In Shrike or the Sparrow BLAKE2b build, open the UTXOs tab (coin control), select the coins and send them to a new address in the same wallet. Check the send screen says replay protected.
  5. Confirm on mempool.guide, then retire the old seeds. Don’t reuse an emptied XBT seed for bitcoin.

Honest about the tools: we found no XBT-specific consolidation tool and no XBT guide on consolidation. The steps above use ordinary send and coin-control features: the UTXOs tab is documented for Sparrow (and in xbt.live’s claim guide), and Shrike is a Sparrow fork that keeps Sparrow’s screens, but we haven’t tested a consolidation in it. Avoid any website or service that offers to consolidate, split or claim coins for you.
