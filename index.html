Secure your XBT in 3 steps

XBT is a separate coin from BTC, on the chain that split from bitcoin on August 8, 2026. If you held bitcoin in your own wallet before then, you already have it.

Never type your seed phrase (your 12 or 24 recovery words) into any website. No real XBT tool will ask.

Which hardware wallets support XBT?

Paperclip Krux and SeedSigner (with community XBT firmware) today, plus spare Trezor and Coldcard devices re-flashed with unofficial firmware. Stock Ledger, Trezor, Coldcard, BitBox02, Jade, Keystone and Passport firmware can’t make XBT’s replay-protected signature yet. Full device table with sources →

What does “in my custody” mean?

Your XBT sits at an address whose seed only you hold, not on an exchange. Best: a hardware wallet that supports XBT. Or Shrike on your computer, with a brand-new seed written on paper. Either way, send a small test first.

Can I turn bitcoin I got after August 8, 2026 into XBT?

Not by splitting. Coins you got after the split exist only on the BTC chain, so there is no XBT “inside” them. You can trade them for XBT on an exchange that has a BTC/XBT market, then withdraw the XBT to your own wallet. Full steps for coins bought after Aug 8.

What is XBT, briefly?

XBT is the chain that kept enforcing the BIP-110 rules (limits on non-payment data) when nearly all SHA-256 miners stayed on the old rules at block 961,632 on August 8, 2026. On August 30, 2026 (block 961,640) it switched its mining to BLAKE2b. The same coin is listed as XBT, BTCB2, Bitcoin BLAKE2b or, on SafeTrade, BIP110. On Kraken and other big platforms, “XBT” means ordinary bitcoin. Why it matters to us: Why XBT.

Sources: xbt.live FAQ, xbt.live history (updated 2026-10-05), BitcoinBIP110.org PSA-260902.

Why the order matters, and what to avoid

The BTC and XBT chains share all history before the split, so the same keys guard coins on both. A careless move can expose your bitcoin to new, unaudited software, or let a transaction sent on one chain be copied (“replayed”) onto the other. Scammers also target fork-coin holders with fake wallets and “claim” sites. This site never asks for a seed or key and has nothing to type one into. See the safety checklist.
