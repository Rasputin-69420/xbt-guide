XBT safety checklist: seeds, fake wallets, claim scams and replay

Quick version

  1. Never type your seed into a website, app or chat. No real XBT tool will ask.
  2. Download wallets only from official release pages, and verify them.
  3. BTC and XBT addresses look identical: keep separate wallets, label them, and send a small test first.

Most losses around a chain split come from a handful of mistakes. Here they are, with how to avoid each one. Use this as a checklist alongside your path on Start here.

Golden rule: never type a seed that still guards bitcoin into new or unfamiliar software. Move your BTC to a fresh wallet first. Nobody needs your seed to “claim” anything.

Your seed

  • A seed that still guards bitcoin never goes into new software, a website, a phone app or a chat. Move the BTC out first.
  • After the split, keep separate seeds for each chain. Better still, make a brand-new XBT-only seed.
  • Back seeds up on paper or steel. No photos, no cloud, no typing them anywhere.

Fake wallets

  • Download only from the project’s official release page, and verify the signature.
  • Watch for look-alike apps and unofficial store listings that use a project’s name.
  • A browser extension or website that asks for your recovery words is a thief.

“Claim” scams

  • Nothing needs to be claimed. Your XBT is already at your addresses.
  • Any site, app or person asking for your seed or private key to claim, unlock or split fork coins is a scammer.
  • The same goes for sites or bots offering to “consolidate”, “recover” or “unlock” XBT for you. You can do every step yourself in your own wallet.
  • Be wary of anyone offering to do the split for you, “support agents” who message you first, and cross-chain splitter tools with no review history.

Replay

  • A normal pre-split bitcoin transaction is valid on both chains. Split it first: with a checked BTC-only coin, an OP_RETURN over 83 bytes, or by moving the XBT first with a SIGHASH_UNIFIED wallet. See the split guide.
  • After any split transaction, check both chains’ explorers to confirm only the chain you meant moved.

Tickers, addresses and exchanges

  • “XBT” on Kraken and other large platforms means ordinary bitcoin. Before any deposit, confirm the network says Bitcoin BLAKE2b.
  • BTC and XBT addresses look identical. Label your wallets clearly and always send a small test first.
  • Type exchange addresses yourself or use a saved bookmark. Look-alike domains and search ads are a common phishing trick.
  • Order books are thin: use limit orders, and buy or sell in pieces. See Exchanges.
  • Don’t leave coins on small exchanges. They can pause withdrawals, delist a coin or fail.
  • Keep only spending money on a phone wallet. Savings go to cold storage.
